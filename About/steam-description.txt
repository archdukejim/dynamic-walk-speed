[h1]Dynamic Walk Speeds[/h1]

A colonist crossing a swept concrete corridor and a colonist wading through knee deep snow move at exactly the same speed in vanilla. This mod makes the ground answer for itself: [b]every cell a pawn steps into is priced by what is actually on it[/b] - the floor under them, the weather over them, the snow and filth in the way, and whether the ground belongs to somebody who wants them dead.

[h2]How it works[/h2]

Five modifiers are read per cell and [b]multiplied together[/b], so a paved hallway in the rain is still faster than mud in the rain. Above [b]1.00x[/b] is faster than vanilla, below it is slower. Every value is a slider and every modifier can be switched off on its own.

[list]
[*][b]Weather[/b] - drizzle barely registers, a blizzard drags.
[*][b]Floors[/b] - the corridor you paved is now worth paving.
[*][b]Creature traction[/b] - and that paved corridor is worth nothing to a guinea pig.
[*][b]Snow and filth[/b] - the game's own snow penalty dialled 0% to 1000%, filth charged by the unit.
[*][b]Hostile territory[/b] - enemy ground puts an edge in every step.
[/list]

[h2]➤ The numbers are all published[/h2]

[b][url=https://github.com/archdukejim/dynamic-walk-speed/wiki/Speed-Table]Read the Speed Table[/url][/b] - every creature on every floor, vanilla and modded ticks side by side, measured in game rather than estimated.

It answers "does flooring my barn help or hurt my muffalo" in one table. [i]It hurts, by about 10%.[/i]

[h2]Creature traction[/h2]

A booted colonist gets everything a paved corridor has to offer. A horse slips on it. A guinea pig, an animal that spent its entire evolutionary history digging its nails into soil, gets nothing from it at all, because there is nothing on smooth tile for those nails to grip.

[b]Traction[/b] decides how much of a floor's bonus a creature can actually use:

[list]
[*][b]1.00x[/b] - takes the full bonus. Colonists, boots on.
[*][b]0.00x[/b] - flooring does nothing for this creature either way.
[*][b]Below zero[/b] - the bonus inverts and the floor becomes a penalty. Your 1.15x paved tile reads as [b]0.89x[/b] to a guinea pig.
[/list]

Natural ground is never touched by traction. This is strictly about what you built.

[h3]Grouped by the game's own data[/h3]

No hand written species list to go stale. Creatures are classified on two axes read straight off the race: [b]body type[/b] from race.body, and [b]size band[/b] from base body size. Measured in game with all DLCs loaded that is [b]20 populated groups over 312 races[/b], and only 4% fall through untreated.

The size axis is what earns its keep: the game calls a 0.2 guinea pig and a 4.0 megasloth both "paws", and they come out with traction of [i]opposite sign[/i].

[b]Modded animals classify themselves[/b] with no patch, including those shipping a custom body. Any individual creature can be ticked open and given its own values.

[h2]Snow, filth and territory[/h2]

[b]Snow does not get a second system bolted onto it.[/b] The game already slows pawns for snow depth, so this scales what the game already does, from [b]0% to 1000%[/b]. At 100% it is vanilla; at 0% the snow penalty is gone entirely, which is worth knowing if you have always found winter tedious. In 1.6 that covers sand too.

[b]Filth is charged by how much is in the cell[/b], counting the thickness the game tracks rather than the number of piles. 1% slower per unit at 100%.

[b]Hostile territory[/b] costs 0.90x by default, triggered by enemy owned tiles, live enemies, or both. [b]Fleeing pawns drop it entirely[/b] - a rout should be a rout, not a slow walk home.

[h2]Footwear[/h2]

Boots add a small traction bonus on manufactured floors, scaled by their [b]quality[/b] on the usual seven step scale. You choose which body part group counts as the traction slot, and every item occupying it gets its own value.

[i]Vanilla ships no footwear, so this sits idle until a mod adds some. Vanilla Apparel Expanded is one that does.[/i]

[b]Experimental, shipped disabled:[/b] barefoot terrain penalties on rough stone, gravel and ice, plus a bare feet mood penalty, a stacking sore feet memory, and a rare foot injury. These are the only parts that add content and damage pawns, and they have not been through real play, so they are opt in until 1.0.

[h2]Settings[/h2]

Five tabs, everything live, nothing requiring a restart: [b]Weather[/b], [b]Floors[/b], [b]Territory and Surface[/b], [b]Creatures[/b] and [b]Footwear[/b]. Every section has its own enable toggle, so this happily runs as a floors only mod, or a traction only mod, with the rest left at vanilla.

[h2]Performance[/h2]

One Harmony postfix, measured at roughly [b]0.001% of the tick budget[/b]: it fires 0.4 to 0.6 times per tick at about 400 ns a call. Everything behind it is precomputed into flat lookup tables, and the hostile pawn sweep is cached per map and faction rather than walking every pawn for every cell.

[url=https://github.com/archdukejim/dynamic-walk-speed/wiki/Test-Results]Full measurements and the 48 case verification run[/url].

[h2]Compatibility[/h2]

[list]
[*][b]RimWorld 1.6.[/b]
[*][b]Combat Extended: yes.[/b] CE changes the move speed [i]stat[/i]; this changes what a [i]cell[/i] costs to enter. Separate layers, no shared patch target.
[*][b]Vehicle Framework: yes.[/b] Detected by type, no assembly reference, no dependency either way.
[*][b]Safe to add or remove mid save.[/b] Nothing is written to your pawns or your map.
[*][b]Modded weather, floors, animals and apparel work out of the box[/b], all read from the game's own defs.
[/list]

[h2]Coming in 1.0: pathfinding preference[/h2]

Right now this changes [i]how fast[/i] a pawn crosses a cell, not [i]which cell it picks[/i]. Your colonists move quickly down a paved corridor, but they will still cut across the mud on the shorter line.

1.0 closes that gap, so humanlike pawns route toward manufactured floors and packed dirt the way people wear a path across a field, while animals carry on going where they please. That is [i]pathfinding[/i] cost rather than [i]movement[/i] cost, and pathfinding is a far hotter piece of the game, so it gets its own release.
